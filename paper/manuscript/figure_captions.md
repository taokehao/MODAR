**Figure 1 | Missing-modality crystal language.**
Crystals are serialized as a CIF-like token sequence (formula, space group, lattice, sites). PXRD and neutron powder patterns are encoded as dense (*d*, *I*) prefixes at placeholder tokens and do not enter the discrete token stream. XRF enters as elemental mass fractions, not as a measured energy spectrum. Any prefix can be dropped; formula-constrained decoding is used only when an exact formula is supplied. One set of weights covers missing-modality combinations and known- or unknown-lattice inference.

*Schematic for Results 3.1. Re-letter against the user drawing if that drawing splits the overview into panels.* Token-length statistics: Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Note 2.

**Figure 6 | Robustness to synthetic experimental artefacts.**
“Noisy” means online synthetic perturbations of simulated inputs (2θ shift, peak broadening, intensity/texture changes, background, counting noise). It does not mean measurements from a diffractometer. All reported rates use *N* = 500 samples per configuration. Clean/noisy ladders compare a clean-trained model plus clean input with a noise-finetuned model plus noisy input; they are not a pure input-noise ablation. **a**, Qualitative schematic of the artefact pipeline; traces are not experimental observations. **b**, Clean versus noisy Top-1 for PXRD+XRF, PXRD+ND+XRF (Multi) and the formula reference, lattice-free and known-lattice. **c**, Corresponding Top-20 values; noisy Top-20 stays at or above 92.4% on the displayed conditions. **d**, Baseline matching (σ = 0.25°) versus deploy matching (σ = 0.40° with ±0.20° shift search) on the same frozen candidate pool. This panel is shown only for noisy, lattice-free inference. **e**, Rietveld-lite refined versus baseline Top-1 within each frozen refinement pool (top 25). A faded XPS-style control is included for completeness and is not a main-text increment (Supplementary Note 5). The large-cell specialist row is a separate candidate pool. **f**, Clean versus noisy large-cell specialist rates and the robustness takeaways: high-information Top-20 recall remains high; deploy matching and Rietveld-lite change ranking; large-cell never-hit structures are not recovered. Deploy and refinement deltas are computed only inside their own frozen pools. Sources: `noisy_robustness_summary.json`, `noisy_rerank_deploy_base_ladder.json`, `refine_rerank_ablation_base_free.json`, `refine_rerank_ablation_bigcell_free.json`. Companion: Supplementary Fig. 9 and Supplementary Note 9.

**Figure 7 | Experimental powders.**
**a**, CNRS (*n* = 126) and RRUFF (*n* = 269) versus RealPXRD-Solver (RPS) under the aligned protocol: paper mix model, PXRD+formula, known lattice, *T* = 1.0 / top-*k* 50 (locked on an independent split), *N* = 100, `F_chgnet` ranking, refine-before-match (lattice-frozen CHGNet and MACE; a hit if the generated or a relaxed structure matches). Plot this work after refinement, reproduced RPS, and RPS paper counts, with paired-bootstrap 95% intervals on this work. CNRS Top-1: 100 [91, 109] versus reproduced 101 (paper 98); Top-20: 118 versus 118 (paper 116). RRUFF Top-1: 221 [208, 233] versus reproduced 199 (paper 212), McNemar *p* = 0.003; Top-20: 246 versus 245 (paper 250). CNRS is statistically indistinguishable from reproduced RPS. RRUFF Top-1 is significantly ahead. RRUFF Top-20 is tied after refinement. Tied cells are argued from intervals, not from a one-structure difference. These four cells do not support a global claim that the model outperforms RPS. Generated-versus-refined counts: Supplementary Table 13.

**b**, [reserved / to be inserted]. Construction protocol only: Supplementary Note 15, Supplementary Figs. 13, 14, Supplementary Table 17.

**c**, Laboratory Cu Kα PXRD of LiCoO2 (Bruker RAW, Q-aligned to λ = 1.54184 Å, 5–80°).
